# Geojit SignalDesk — Editorial Category & AI Writing Framework (v1.0)

**Status:** Proposed editorial standard for internal review, **not activated in the AI endpoint or Studio UI**  
**Owner:** Geojit Marketing / Editorial; subject to Research, Marcom and Compliance approval  
**Purpose:** Convert financial information into useful, accurate, recognisably Geojit editorial communications. Not simply to rewrite headlines or imitate a competitor.

## 1. Editorial proposition

**Make financial developments easier to understand.**

Every piece should deliver at least one reader benefit: **know what changed; understand what happened; learn a concept; read the relevant facts; or make sense of a financial habit**. The editorial test is: *“Would a reader be better informed after reading this, even if they do not invest with Geojit?”*

**Voice:** Trusted, measured, clear, accessible, factual, helpful and curious where appropriate.  
**Avoid:** Salesy, urgent, sensational, imitative, condescending, speculative, cryptic, formulaic and overloaded with financial jargon.

Competitor screenshot observations supplied for this exercise:
- **Zerodha**: Changes to exchange/trading processes explained with dates and before/after details.
- **ICICI Direct**: Market history, earnings numbers, sector performance and research-led comparisons.
- **Angel One**: Beginner-first concept teaching, e.g. breaking down a long mutual-fund name.
- **Groww**: News with curiosity hooks and progressive “here's why” explanations.
- **Motilal Oswal**: Relatable money amounts and narrative framing around financial habits.

These are **patterns** from the examples, not an exhaustive competitor performance audit or instructions to reproduce their wording.

## 2. The six editorial categories

One item has **one primary editorial category**, with optional topic tags and an optional secondary category. *Category* describes **reader purpose**, not the same thing as *writing format*. For example, a regulator circular can be a short news update or a thread, but its category remains **Market & Regulatory Updates**.

### C1 — Market & Regulatory Updates
**Reader promise:** *Know what changed, when it applies and who it affects.*  
**Typical stories:** RBI/SEBI releases, exchange procedures, trading-session timings, investor-protection notices, disclosure changes and material policy developments.  
**Source requirements:** Primary RBI/SEBI/exchange circular or official release; effective date and scope, if the post states either. When a story originates in secondary news, verify the underlying primary document.  
**Preferred opening:** Name the institution and specific action. “SEBI has revised [specific rule] for [affected participants].” Only use this formulation if the circular confirms it.  
**Writing structure:** (1) Institution/action → (2) exact change, condition or effective date → (3) practical clarification or before/after comparison supported by the release.  
**Permitted audience value:** Procedural understanding, eligibility, scope, steps and exceptions stated in the primary source.  
**Never:** Invent implementation dates, say “all investors” when scope is limited, imply a price impact, predict benefits, turn routine announcements into urgent alerts, or misstate proposal/consultation as an implemented rule.  
**Recommended formats:** One-post alert; “Earlier / Now” table; 3–5-post explainer thread for complex rules.  
**Withhold/ask:** Cannot establish whether the rule is proposed/final, its effective date where material, or the relevant population.

### C2 — Market Intelligence & Context
**Reader promise:** *Understand an observed market development without being told what to trade.*  
**Typical stories:** Index/sector movements, valuation and historical comparisons, macroeconomic indicators, market breadth, rates, liquidity and major market trends.  
**Source requirements:** Dated observed metrics from an authorised market-data source, index provider, exchange, RBI or a verified Geojit Research publication; definition, time window and comparison basis.  
**Preferred opening:** The actual observation with time/metric, not “The market is interesting today.”  
**Writing structure:** (1) What the data shows → (2) period, comparison or contrast → (3) limited supported context; explicitly distinguish correlation from cause.  
**Permitted audience value:** Benchmark definitions, comparisons, historical ranges and how to read figures.  
**Never:** Describe a correction as “healthy,” claim investor sentiment or motivations without evidence, promise a rebound, infer a sector rotation from a single day's moves, cherry-pick history, or create recommendations from descriptive data.  
**Recommended formats:** Numbers-led post; small, labelled chart; limited 3-post context thread with methodology.  
**Withhold/ask:** No precise timestamp, data provenance, comparable denominator or historical methodology where required; only an external author's unsupported interpretation is available.

### C3 — Company & Earnings
**Reader promise:** *Know what a company announced and the meaningful verified figures.*  
**Typical stories:** Quarterly results, official corporate announcements, dividends, management changes, major orders, IPO filings and corporate actions.  
**Source requirements:** Exchange filing or official company announcement; exact reporting period, metric, amount and units; separate headline from percentage calculations.  
**Preferred opening:** Company + disclosed event + period.  
**Writing structure:** (1) Company/event → (2) two or three material reported figures with correct units and comparison bases → (3) caveat or next disclosed milestone only if supplied.  
**Permitted audience value:** Factual results, changes and what the disclosure specifically reports.  
**Never:** “Stock to buy/watch” recommendations; price targets; fabricated market reaction; misleading YoY vs QoQ; merging consolidated and standalone results; overstating an order win as booked revenue; implying a draft IPO filing is a confirmed listing.  
**Recommended formats:** Single fact-led post; two-row results card; audited “Three figures” thread.  
**Withhold/ask:** Missing period, units, source filing, comparison denominator or exact corporate-action status.

### C4 — Financial Education
**Reader promise:** *Understand one confusing financial idea in ordinary language.*  
**Typical stories:** What Direct/Regular/Growth mean, SIP vs lump sum mechanics, demat basics, F&O terminology, interest and compounding, risk and diversification.  
**Source requirements:** Geojit-approved evergreen knowledge base, regulator/exchange/investor-education source or approved primary product definitions; clear limitations and relevant exceptions.  
**Preferred opening:** A recognisable user question or confusing term, followed by a direct answer.  
**Writing structure:** (1) Simple question/term → (2) plain-English explanation → (3) one relatable illustration or comparison → (4) caveat if the simplification could mislead.  
**Permitted audience value:** Foundational financial literacy and reducing the effort to understand jargon.  
**Never:** Present general education as personalised advice; imply an asset class is suitable for everyone; guarantee SIP returns; infer tax consequences without jurisdiction, date and circumstances; call mutual funds “safe” or “risk-free.”  
**Recommended formats:** Short explainer; glossary card; two-part beginner thread.  
**Withhold/ask:** Source definition not approved or simplification would omit a material risk, condition or cost.

### C5 — Money & Wealth Habits
**Reader promise:** *Connect a financial principle to a realistic everyday choice.*  
**Typical stories:** Saving habits, budgeting, emergency reserves, patience, consistency, goal planning, difference between frequency and amount of contributions.  
**Source requirements:** Approved financial-education principles; accurate arithmetic and explicit contribution amounts, period, assumptions, illustrative return (if any), and important risks.  
**Preferred opening:** A relatable money decision or simple comparison, not guilt or a claim about the reader's lifestyle.  
**Writing structure:** (1) Everyday situation → (2) show the actual money flow/assumptions → (3) explain the financial concept without giving a prescriptive plan.  
**Permitted audience value:** Understand habits, trade-offs and the difference between investing more, investing earlier and investment performance.  
**Never:** Compare ₹5,000 per month with ₹5,000 per year as though contribution totals were identical; promise compounding outcomes; shame spending; prescribe a savings percentage for everyone; imply SIP automatically beats inflation.  
**Recommended formats:** Narrative post; assumption-labelled calculator graphic; 3–4-post illustrative thread.  
**Withhold/ask:** Unclear whether amounts, duration, inflation, return assumption or fees are comparable.

### C6 — Explainers & Deep Dives
**Reader promise:** *Understand why a mechanism works, step by step.*  
**Typical stories:** Auction mechanics, opening/closing price discovery, market plumbing, IPO process, bond-yield concepts, why a rule creates certain procedural steps.  
**Source requirements:** Primary procedural document, approved technical reference or clearly corroborated official mechanism; the source must support **each** claimed step.  
**Preferred opening:** A meaningful “How does [mechanism] work?” or verified counterintuitive fact that motivates the explanation.  
**Writing structure:** (1) What question are we answering? → (2) three to five ordered factual steps → (3) simple example/visual → (4) boundary or exception.  
**Permitted audience value:** Mechanism and causal explanation **only where the source actually establishes the mechanism**.  
**Never:** Convert an unexplained market anomaly into an invented cause; disguise guesses as a “why”; turn the thread into a financial recommendation or sensational revelation; oversimplify mandatory technical conditions.  
**Recommended formats:** 3–5-post thread; annotated flow chart; before/after or step diagram.  
**Withhold/ask:** The event is known but the mechanism or supporting source is not available.

## 3. Category versus output format — design separation

The category defines **what value** the reader gets. The output format defines **how** that value is conveyed.

| Format / mode | Primary purpose | Typical categories |
|---|---|---|
| **News Update** | Verifiable facts, who/what/when | C1, C2, C3 |
| **Simply Explained** | One concept, clear analogy | C4, C6 |
| **Why It Matters** | Only specifically supported relevance | C1, C2, C3; sometimes C4 |
| **Data & Context** | Defined metric + clear comparator | C2, C3, C5 |
| **Thread Builder** | Progressive multi-part explanation | C1, C4, C5, C6 |
| **Money & Wealth Habits** | Decision/behaviour narrative | C5 |

**Not six compulsory variations per story.** The system should suggest one strongest primary output and at most one optional alternative. If the input cannot substantiate a “Why It Matters,” return an empty explanation with a request for evidence.

## 4. Universal AI rules — apply to every category

1. **Geojit voice, not provider voice:** Describe verified developments directly. The publisher name remains in **source/reference metadata**; never frame a tweet as “MarketsMojo observes…” merely because the story came from MarketsMojo. Keep any legally required publisher attribution outside the narrative in the approved format. **Do not erase required attribution.**
2. **No invented Geojit Research:** “Geojit expects…”, analyst views, forecasts, price targets and recommendations require actual authorised Geojit Research content and the corresponding compliance process. Source commentary alone never becomes Geojit opinion.
3. **One useful idea:** Lead with a clear actor/fact, a relevant question or an exact comparison. Do not write hollow conclusions such as “investors should stay informed,” “this may create opportunities,” or “reflecting sentiment” without corroboration.
4. **Evidence over fluency:** Every number, date, comparison, quotation, rule, cause, population, conclusion and chart label must trace to source evidence. A fluent model response is not proof. Flag missing evidence.
5. **Accurate time:** Source publication date is not the same as retrieval time. Never say “today”, “latest”, “has just”, “new rule applies”, or imply current prices when dates are absent or stale. Verify planned-versus-effective dates.
6. **Clear, restrained accessibility:** Prefer short sentences and ordinary words. Explain necessary financial jargon once. No clickbait, urgency, fear, FOMO, guaranteed outcomes, unsupported adjectives, unnecessary emojis, or artificially youthful language.
7. **Careful curiosity:** A question is allowed only if the content answers it accurately and immediately, without hiding a key caveat or exaggerating the claim.
8. **No advice by accident:** General information is not personalised advice. Avoid buy/sell/hold cues, implied suitability, return projections or misleading “stocks to watch” claims unless authorised by a separately approved research and compliance workflow.
9. **Rights and sources:** Use only permitted inputs. Do not scrape third-party sites, reproduce article prose/images/tables, use proprietary ratings or publish POC content without explicit rights. Keep original URL, publisher, date, evidence quality and editorial notes in the internal record.
10. **Exact arithmetic and context:** Distinguish YoY/QoQ, basis points versus percentages, crores versus lakhs, cumulative versus annual, returns versus amounts invested, consolidated versus standalone results, and published versus effective dates.
11. **Formatting that serves readers:** A thread, chart or table must add genuine clarity. Use one clear hook followed by logical progression; not forced multi-post cliffhangers. Avoid excessive hashtags; at most two meaningful hashtags and only if appropriate.
12. **Complete, accountable outputs:** Never clip a sentence or word. The **current app** enforces approximately **165 characters for each AI-generated variant**; X allows a longer standard post. A future format-specific character budget must account for final hashtags, mandatory disclosures and source links. Do not change the live character limit until implemented and tested.
13. **Review remains human:** AI writes *drafts*. A human opens the original, verifies figures/context, edits, checks required approvals/disclosures and decides whether and how to publish. SignalDesk does not autopublish to X.

### Mandatory editorial stop conditions

Respond with **“More evidence needed”** (not invented content) where any of these applies:
- Only a secondary headline is available for a claim requiring a regulator circular, company filing or original dataset.
- An implied reason, effect, sentiment, return or “opportunity” cannot be established from the evidence.
- Historical/data comparisons have incompatible periods, unknown denominators or unclear methodology.
- Key dates/status (proposed vs final; filing vs completed event) or material numbers are missing.
- A third-party licence does not allow processing or publication; **do not treat a POC as permission**.
- A claim would require a Geojit Research recommendation or Compliance approval not supplied.

### Source strength rubric

| Level | Input evidence | Allowed output |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Primary verified** | Official circular/filing or approved Geojit Research report, verified by editor | Fact post; supported explanation; relevant data graphic/thread |
| **B — Verified multiple details** | Authorised source metadata and editor-checked corroborating facts | Constrained facts and short educational context; no unverified causes |
| **C — Headline only / unverified** | RSS headline, snippet, manual POC entry | Internal factual draft only if clearly identifiable; withhold deeper implications |
| **D — Incomplete / rights unclear** | Ambiguous, undated, contradictory, or unlicensed content | Ask for the precise missing evidence; do not generate publishable copy |

Evidence level is **not a prediction of truth**. It is the minimum editorial basis for a candidate output; the editor still verifies source content.

## 5. Classification and writing flow (future implementation)

1. **Read evidence**, not just headline keywords: source type, title, article date, facts, numeric metrics, regulator/company, comparison period, topic, rights status.
2. **Identify reader intent** and classify one primary category (C1–C6), with optional secondary tag; return a short human-readable reason. The editor may override.
3. **Use simple routing priority where categories overlap**:
   - If the news is a rule/change with verified applicability → C1.
   - If the core development is a named company's filing/result → C3.
   - If the core value is a dated market observation/metric → C2.
   - If teaching one term/definition → C4.
   - If teaching a financial behaviour/trade-off → C5.
   - If explaining a complex multi-step mechanism → C6.
   - **Resolve by reader promise, not by first keyword**; a SEBI circular discussed for its step-by-step auction mechanics may be C6 with a C1 tag.
4. **Check evidence and rights** against category-specific requirements. If insufficient, ask for specific facts or limit output to an internal draft. Never auto-fill missing data.
5. **Choose a format**: default one concise post if clear, a short comparison if evidence is numeric, or a thread only if the subject needs ordered steps.
6. **Generate** primary post and at most one optional angle; explain what evidence supports any “Why It Matters.” Provide an internal source-evidence note, not a made-up citation.
7. **Validate** each claim, number, date, source, tone, format, disclosure and total length. Fail closed if a substantive rule is violated.
8. **Human approval** is mandatory. Manual final X publishing remains separate.

## 6. Editorial examples — illustrative prompts, not verified current news

- **C1:** “An exchange has changed when certain orders may be modified. What changed, from which date and for which instruments?” → Need official effective circular before generating.
- **C2:** “A sector rose while the wider index fell. State the exact period, index return and sector return; do not assert why.”
- **C3:** “A company released Q2 earnings. Show revenue, profit and comparison basis, as disclosed in its filing.”
- **C4:** “What do the words in a mutual-fund scheme name tell you?” → Define AMC, plan type and option separately.
- **C5:** “₹5,000 monthly vs ₹5,000 annually” → State first that the contribution totals differ; do not credit the full gap to compounding.
- **C6:** “How is an opening price discovered in an auction?” → Three to five steps taken from an actual exchange rulebook.

## 7. Editorial QA scorecard

Score five dimensions **0–2 each** (total 10). Intended for reviewer judgement, **not** automatic factual approval:

| Dimension | 0 = fails | 1 = needs work | 2 = passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Truth and provenance** | Unsupported / inaccurate claim | Partially evidenced | Every material assertion traceable |
| **Reader usefulness** | Generic or irrelevant | Some value, unclear | Answers a real reader question |
| **Geojit voice** | Hype / provider voice / implied advice | Inconsistent | Composed, clear and independent |
| **Clarity and structure** | Confusing / incomplete | Long or dense | Natural, self-contained, well sequenced |
| **Rights and compliance** | No rights or required approval | Pending checks | Confirmed permitted use and approvals |

**Gate:** Any **0** in truth/provenance or rights/compliance blocks publication, regardless of total. **8/10** is a possible editorial quality target for a pilot, never a substitute for fact-checking.

## 8. Pilot editorial mix (guidance, not a quota)

For a possible six-post news/education day: 1 market/regulatory, 1 market intelligence, 1 company/earnings, 1 financial education, 1 explainer/deep dive, 1 money/wealth habit. **Do not force six posts** on days without verified worthwhile material. Availability and rights will determine the mix.

## 9. Implementation acceptance criteria — *after* framework approval

- An editor can see/edit the suggested primary category **and why** it was selected.
- Every category has its own prompt/evidence gate. No generic “investor sentiment” or routine “stay informed” filler.
- AI only offers formats feasible with the source evidence. Thread content is structured and checked post-by-post.
- The app recognises unverified or undated stories and blocks false freshness language.
- Publisher reference is retained internally and contractual attribution is respected, while the post itself uses Geojit's editorial voice.
- Output has no clipped clauses/words. Final X length checks include text, hashtags, source URL and mandatory disclosure.
- POC-only MarketsMojo content continues to be internal-review-only until permissions are granted.
- User testing demonstrates improvement in accuracy, usefulness, editing time and editorial confidence, not just increased post counts.
